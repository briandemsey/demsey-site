The Turkey in the Middle of the Road

Brian R. Demsey | November 2026

← Back to Articles

My first job in the actuarial world was as a trainee at Marsh & McLennan. That winter I wore a topcoat and a fedora to the office, and in an effort to fit in, I accepted an invitation from an older colleague to go turkey shooting.

It was the fall of 1964. We drove about thirty miles out of town, parked my VW Bug, and waited. After a while, a turkey walked out of the woods and stood in the middle of the road.

My colleague got out of the car and took a shot. Miss. I got out, steadied my aim, and fired. Miss.

The turkey looked at us, plainly amused, and flipped us the bird. The middle finger, from a bird. Then it crossed to the other side of the road and into the woods. It didn't fly. It didn't run. It walked, ever so calmly, to where it was going.

And here we are today, and turkey season is open again. This year, I'm the turkey in the middle of the road.

The woods to my left and right are filled with peril. The red are easy targets. The blue are bleeding hearts. Both are equally dangerous. In the center I am free to choose among the broods, but there is no party I can call home.

Happy Thanksgiving. And make peace with the sister-in-law who is too stupid to see how smart you are.
